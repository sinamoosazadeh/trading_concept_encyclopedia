# Canonical entry standard

Each entry should contain:

- stable ID and canonical name;
- aliases, language, category, and concise summary;
- neutral definition and operationalization when meaningful;
- prerequisites, inputs, decision timing, parameters, and edge cases where applicable;
- contexts and known limits;
- claims with explicit types, scopes, statuses, and supporting or contrary source IDs;
- related concepts with relationship type and rationale;
- lifecycle status, evidence status, review notes, and change log.

## Writing standard

Use direct language. Define specialist terms at first use when necessary. Separate observable properties from interpretations and trade hypotheses. Replace ambiguous words such as “strong”, “valid”, “significant”, or “high probability” with measurable criteria, or explicitly classify them as discretionary judgement.

Do not universalize a concept from one venue, asset, timeframe, or market regime. State the conditions of applicability and identify unresolved terminology.

## Operational descriptions

Where a concept has mechanical rules, specify the information available at the decision point, lookback window, thresholds, bar-close or intrabar behavior, tie-breaking rules, handling of missing data, and invalidation conditions. Rules that cannot be reproduced consistently should be marked as discretionary or under-specified.

## Related concepts

Use explicit relationship labels such as synonym, broader_than, narrower_than, prerequisite_for, alternative_to, often_confused_with, or implemented_as. A link must not imply equivalence unless equivalence is intended.

## Change log

Every substantive change should record a date, change summary, and rationale. When a record is merged, renamed, or deprecated, preserve a redirect or replacement ID instead of silently erasing its history.
