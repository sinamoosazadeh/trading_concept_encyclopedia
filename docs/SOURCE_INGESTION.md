# Source ingestion and provenance

## Goal

Ingestion is a traceable transformation from an external item to metadata, claims, and evidence notes. It is not permission to copy, redistribute, or treat every statement as fact.

## Workflow

1. **Discover** a candidate source and record how it was found.
2. **Identify** author/creator, title, publication or release date, publisher, persistent identifier, URL, accessed date, version, and source type where available.
3. **Check provenance**: confirm that the source is authentic and that the referenced artifact is the correct edition or revision.
4. **Check rights**: establish whether quotation, extraction, transformation, storage, and redistribution are permitted. When unclear, store only bibliographic metadata and original-link references until resolved.
5. **Assess relevance**: map the source to specific statements or definitions, not just a topic label.
6. **Extract claims** in neutral, atomic wording without strengthening the source's conclusion.
7. **Record limitations** such as non-peer-reviewed status, small sample, conflicts of interest, narrow instruments, unknown methodology, or missing data.
8. **Seek counter-evidence** and alternative definitions.
9. **Review** whether the entry wording accurately reflects the cited material.
10. **Version** any correction or source update while preserving provenance.

## Source versioning

For mutable web pages, repositories, APIs, and documentation, record access date and version or commit where possible. For papers and books, distinguish publication year, edition, online posting date, and later correction dates. A URL can redirect or change; add a DOI, archive identifier, release tag, or content hash when available and lawful.

## Source quality dimensions

Assess separately:
- authenticity and traceability;
- relevance to the specific claim;
- method transparency;
- sample or scope adequacy;
- independence and potential conflicts;
- recency when facts or specifications are mutable;
- reproducibility;
- directness of evidence;
- known counter-evidence.

Do not compress these dimensions into a single unsupported credibility score.

## Web and practitioner sources

Practitioner sources can help discover terminology and generate hypotheses. They do not alone establish statistical edge. Marketing claims, testimonial screenshots, isolated chart examples, and unattributed statistics should be treated as weak evidence until independently substantiated.

## Extraction discipline

Keep quotations short and necessary. Prefer paraphrase with precise attribution. Do not store full copyrighted chapters, paid course content, or large scraped collections without permission. Never invent bibliographic fields to satisfy a schema; use null where the schema allows it or document the missing information.

## Audit trail

Each claim must identify supporting and contrary source IDs independently. If a source is removed, superseded, inaccessible, or retracted, preserve a record of that status and update the affected claims rather than silently deleting the source.
