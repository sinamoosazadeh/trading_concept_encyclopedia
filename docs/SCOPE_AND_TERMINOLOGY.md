# Scope and terminology

## Purpose

This project records trading-related concepts in a structured, traceable, reviewable form. It is a reference and research infrastructure, not a catalogue of guaranteed profitable signals.

## In scope

The encyclopedia may document market structure, price and volume observations, chart and candlestick patterns, technical indicators, derived features, signal definitions, setups, strategy hypotheses, execution methods, portfolio concepts, risk controls, market microstructure, data methodology, and evaluation methods.

## Required distinctions

- **Observation:** a description of data that can be identified without claiming a trade should be taken.
- **Concept:** a defined term or recurring phenomenon; the term alone carries no claim of predictive value.
- **Feature:** a computed input represented with a reproducible calculation.
- **Hypothesis:** a claim that could be contradicted by evidence.
- **Signal:** a rule that maps information available at a decision time to an output.
- **Setup:** a conditional market state or rule set used to identify a possible trade opportunity.
- **Strategy:** a complete or near-complete decision policy, including entry, exit, sizing, and relevant constraints.
- **Execution method:** a procedure for turning an intended order into actual fills.
- **Risk control:** a rule intended to constrain exposure, loss, or operational failure.
- **Empirical result:** the result of a specified protocol applied to specified data. It does not automatically generalize to other assets, periods, venues, or regimes.

A record may link several types, but it must not blur them into one term.

## Out of scope

A label list alone is not a verified encyclopedia. Inclusion does not constitute investment advice, a recommendation to trade, or evidence of positive expectancy. The repository does not promise complete coverage of every trading tradition or vocabulary.

## Unit of documentation

Prefer one concept per canonical record. Split concepts when definitions, operational rules, evidential status, or applications materially differ. Merge records only when the underlying meaning is equivalent, documenting redirects and the merge rationale.

## Language

English is the initial machine-readable canonical language for IDs and schema keys. Persian and other terminology may be stored as aliases or translations. Translations must preserve uncertainty and scope rather than silently strengthening a claim.
